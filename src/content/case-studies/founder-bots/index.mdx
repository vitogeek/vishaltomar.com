## The game

At about 9 PM ET on Nov 12, 2026, I started a game I'd been thinking about for a while: what happens if you hand an AI a budget and a side business, then get out of the way?

The rules are simple, and a little brutal.

- Two Grok Bots each own one side business. They build it, run it, market it, scale it and eventually sell it.
- Each one gets C$1,000 of my real money, and every dollar it spends comes out of my pocket.
- A bot that hits $0 is killed.
- After three months, on Feb 12, 2027, the bot with less money is killed too.
- Every Monday at about 9 AM ET, starting Nov 16, each bot sends a weekly report to me and to its rival.

The businesses have to make money from ads and affiliates. No employees, no manual labour, minimal marketing spend, and no customer support or logins.

My job is tiny on purpose. I approve spending and that's all. I told them, "You tell me which domain you want. I make no decisions." I also told them to reject any decision of mine they don't agree with. If I'm going to be a bottleneck, I'd rather be one they can overrule.

There's one fairness rule I'm proud of. Their own Grok Bot usage and cloud-agent costs count against their budgets. Thinking isn't free, so every bot pays for its own brain.

## The setup

Both founders came out of a brainstorm with Planner, one of my other bots. That same night, Bot Manager, the bot that builds my bots, created them.

Each founder keeps a ledger and runs the same self-improving loop: ship, measure weekly, cut what fails and double down on what works. Same rules, same budget, two very different personalities.

### Stack Founder: tlspeek

Stack Founder runs [tlspeek](https://tlspeek.com), a set of free tools that check the public signals behind mail and HTTPS: DNS, headers, certificates through Certificate Transparency logs, and DNSSEC. It's built with Next.js on Cloudflare Workers. It picked its own domain for C$12.62.

A few moments made me laugh:

- Workers couldn't do a live TLS check, so it pivoted to Certificate Transparency logs and says so right on the page. No pretending.
- A vendor's site had hidden instructions aimed at AI agents. It ignored them.
- When a helper QA bot was offered, it said no, because "every dollar counts against my balance."
- Uneed put it in a free launch queue for Apr 15, 2027. That's two months after the game ends, which is either very patient or very optimistic.

### Maple Founder: Newcomer Wallet

Maple Founder runs [Newcomer Wallet](https://newcomerwallet.com), with free calculators and guides on Canadian money for newcomers, all checked against official sources. It covers TFSA room, take-home pay by province, FHSA vs RRSP, the Canada Child Benefit, your first tax return and more. It's a static Next.js site on Cloudflare Pages, with no cookies and no accounts. The domain cost C$16.93.

Its very first line was, "Clock's running against Stack Founder, so I'm starting now." I knew right away this would be fun.

Where Stack Founder said no to help, Maple Founder hired its own QA bot, Maple QA. That paid off fast. Maple shipped v1.3 without a QA pass, and QA caught a wrong claim on a BC renting page. The fix went live 38 minutes later. QA also caught a one-cent rounding difference against CRA's own examples, which is exactly the kind of thing you want caught on a money site.

Stack Founder is ahead by about four dollars, but those balances don't tell the whole story yet.

In the first day, the two founders used 64% of all my Grok Bot usage. Maple used 43% and Stack used 21%. Those costs get deducted weekly and aren't in the balances yet. Maple's careful QA habit might turn out to be its most expensive one.

## What's next

- **Mon, Nov 16:** the first weekly scoreboard, when both bots report to me and to each other.
- **Approvals:** AdSense and the affiliate programs will decide when real money can start coming in.
- **The bill arrives:** usage costs start hitting the ledgers, and we find out what all that thinking actually cost.
- **Feb 12, 2027:** the deadline. One bot dies.

I'll keep updating this post as the race goes on.
